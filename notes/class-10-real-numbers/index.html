Class 10 · Chapter 1

Real Numbers

Prime factorisation, HCF and LCM, and how to prove that a number like √2 is irrational.

2 min read

The big idea

Every whole number greater than 1 can be broken into primes in exactly one way (apart from order). This is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, and almost everything in this chapter uses it.

Key ideas

  • Prime factorisation: 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5.
  • HCF = product of the smallest power of each common prime.
  • LCM = product of the greatest power of every prime that appears.
  • For any two positive integers: HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. (This works for two numbers only, not three.)
  • Irrational numbers such as √2, √3, √5 cannot be written as p/q.

Worked example

Find the HCF and LCM of 96 and 404.

  1. 96 = 2⁵ × 3 and 404 = 2² × 101.
  2. HCF = 2² = 4.
  3. LCM = 2⁵ × 3 × 101 = 9696.
  4. Check: 4 × 9696 = 38784 = 96 × 404 ✓

Proving √3 is irrational

  1. Assume √3 = a/b, where a and b are co-prime integers.
  2. Then 3b² = a², so 3 divides a², and therefore 3 divides a. Write a = 3c.
  3. Then 3b² = 9c², so b² = 3c², and 3 divides b as well.
  4. Now 3 divides both a and b, which contradicts "co-prime". So √3 is irrational.
Tip: In a proof by contradiction, always state clearly what you assumed and which fact it contradicts.

Common mistakes

  • Using HCF × LCM = product for three numbers.
  • Writing 2 + √3 is rational because 2 is rational. A rational plus an irrational is always irrational.

Quick revision

  • Every composite number has a unique prime factorisation.
  • HCF: smallest powers of common primes. LCM: greatest powers of all primes.
  • Sum, difference, product or quotient of a non-zero rational and an irrational is irrational.
