Vector Algebra

Quantities with both size and direction: components, dot product, cross product and their geometric meaning.

2 min read

The big idea

A vector has both a magnitude and a direction, like a displacement of 5 km towards north. We write it in components as a = a₁î + a₂ĵ + a₃k̂, and its magnitude is |a| = √(a₁² + a₂² + a₃²).

Key ideas

  • Unit vector along a: â = a / |a|.
  • Position vector of P(x, y, z): xî + yĵ + zk̂. Vector AB = (position of B) − (position of A).
  • Section formula: the point dividing AB internally in m : n has position vector (mb + na)/(m + n).

Dot product (scalar)

  • a · b = |a||b| cos θ = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃
  • a ⊥ b exactly when a · b = 0.
  • Projection of a on b = (a · b) / |b|.

Cross product (vector)

  • |a × b| = |a||b| sin θ, direction perpendicular to both a and b.
  • a × b = determinant with rows (î, ĵ, k̂), (a₁, a₂, a₃), (b₁, b₂, b₃).
  • a × b = −(b × a). a ∥ b exactly when a × b = 0.
  • Area of parallelogram = |a × b|; area of triangle = ½|a × b|.

Worked example

Find the angle between a = î + ĵ − k̂ and b = î − ĵ + k̂.

  1. a · b = 1 − 1 − 1 = −1.
  2. |a| = |b| = √3.
  3. cos θ = −1/3, so θ = cos⁻¹(−1/3).
Tip: Write the cross product as a 3 × 3 determinant every time. Doing it in your head is where sign errors happen.

Common mistakes

  • Writing a × b = b × a.
  • Forgetting that î · î = 1 but î × î = 0.

Quick revision

  • a · b = 0: perpendicular. a × b = 0: parallel.
  • Triangle area = ½|AB × AC|.
